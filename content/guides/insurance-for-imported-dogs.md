An overseas rescue dog comes with a particular insurance challenge: you often don't know its full history. It may be carrying an infection picked up abroad, have an old injury that never healed quite right, or have a condition that hasn't shown itself yet. Good insurance, bought at the right moment, is how you stop an unknown past from becoming an unaffordable future.

::callout{type="info"}
The most important decision isn't which insurer — it's **when**. Insure your dog from the day it arrives, before any symptom appears. A condition your dog shows signs of before cover starts, or during the first 14 days, is normally treated as pre-existing and excluded, whichever policy you choose.
::

## Why lifetime cover usually wins for a rescue

UK pet insurance broadly comes in four shapes:

- **Accident-only** — cheapest, covers injuries but not illness. Rarely enough for a dog with an unknown medical background.
- **Time-limited** — covers a condition up to a set limit or for a set period, usually 12 months, then stops. Chronic conditions don't respect the calendar.
- **Maximum-benefit** — covers each condition up to a fixed money limit, with no time limit, but once that limit is spent the condition isn't covered again.
- **Lifetime** — the most comprehensive: an annual limit that **resets each year** when you renew, with conditions carried over rather than excluded.

For a rescue where you can't predict what's coming, **lifetime cover** is usually the right call. The extra premium buys you continued cover for chronic conditions — allergies, joint problems, digestive issues — that a cheaper policy would drop after a year, right when you need it most.

## When cover can actually start

For an imported dog, arrival day is the date that matters, and there are two rules that make it so.

**Cover may not begin until the dog is with you.** Petplan's Covered For Life booklet says that if your pet isn't yet in your possession when the policy starts, cover doesn't begin until you take possession, and anything that happens before then isn't covered. You can sort the policy out in the week before transport, but set the start date to the day your dog arrives.

**The first days are a waiting period.** In the three policy booklets we read:

| Insurer (booklet) | Accidents not covered | Illness not covered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Petplan — Covered For Life (June 2024) | — | Symptoms in the first 14 days |
| Animal Friends — Lifetime (2025) | First 2 days | Signs first noticed in the first 14 days |
| ManyPets — Handbook (Nov 2024) | First 48 hours | Illness in the first 14 days |

Getting the timing wrong costs more than the waiting period itself:

- **Petplan** won't cover an illness that shows symptoms in the first 14 days. It also won't cover any later illness with the same symptoms or diagnosis, even years later.
- **Animal Friends** says a condition first noticed during the waiting period isn't covered, even if you wait until afterwards to get it treated.
- **ManyPets** treats claims declined for the waiting period as pre-existing until the dog has been on cover for more than 12 months.

Journeys from Romania, Cyprus and elsewhere often take days on the road, and a new dog is under a lot of stress. Every day of delay adds to the risk that something appears before your cover is really working.

## The unknown-history problem

Three things make imported rescues different from a UK puppy bought from a breeder:

1. **Pre-existing conditions are judged on symptoms, not diagnosis.** The Financial Ombudsman describes a pre-existing condition as one your pet had signs and symptoms of before the policy started, or that appeared within the first 14 days. When a claim is disputed, it looks at the policy terms, your pet's medical history, and what you knew or should have known when you took the policy out. Give the insurer every piece of paperwork the rescue gave you. Some insurers don't simply refuse all pre-existing conditions: Petplan says it may place a permanent exclusion, a temporary one that can be removed after a symptom-free period, or none at all if the condition has fully resolved.
2. **Estimated age.** Many dogs arrive with a best-guess date of birth, and insurers use it. Animal Friends, for example, says it uses breed, weight and date of birth to work out your premium, and that if your vet confirms different details, your premium may go up or down. Age also affects what you can buy and what you pay when you claim. Animal Friends adds a co-payment (a share of each vet bill) for dogs from age 8, and Petplan says its Covered For Life policy is usually only available for dogs under 8. Get your vet's estimate of age on record at the first check.
3. **No baseline health record.** Without puppyhood vet notes, the first UK vet visit becomes the baseline. Book a health check soon after arrival. It's good for the dog, and it records your dog's condition at the start of cover.

## Diseases from abroad: read the exclusions

Dogs from southern and eastern Europe can carry infections that aren't normally found in the UK. Langford Vets (the University of Bristol's veterinary hospital) recommends testing imported dogs for *Brucella canis*, *Leishmania infantum*, *Ehrlichia canis*, heartworm, *Babesia*, *Anaplasma* and *Hepatozoon canis*. In a 2017 survey of more than 3,000 owners of imported rescue dogs, owners reported that 14.8% (79 of 533) of the dogs tested for *Leishmania* had a positive result.

This affects insurance in three ways:

- **Some policies exclude leishmaniasis completely.** Animal Friends' lifetime policy booklet lists leishmaniasis among the conditions it doesn't cover, alongside rabies and sexually transmitted diseases. Search the booklet of any policy you're considering for "leishmaniasis" and for "exotic" or "notifiable" diseases before you buy.
- **Infections caught abroad may count as pre-existing.** Heathfield Vets notes that if an imported dog has leishmaniasis, which can only have been caught before it came to the UK, an insurer is likely to treat it as pre-existing and may not cover diagnosis or treatment. Heathfield suggests phoning an insurer before you commit to ask whether it would cover this.
- **Screening tests usually aren't covered.** Langford says insurance is unlikely to pay for screening tests in healthy animals, so budget for them yourself. Timing matters too: antibodies to *Brucella canis* can take up to 3 months after exposure to become detectable, and only around half of dogs have detectable *Leishmania* antibodies 5 months after exposure, with some taking up to 2 years. Ask your vet when to test.

For dogs from Romania, there's one extra check before travel. Since 7 October 2025, dogs commercially imported from Romania, which includes rescue dogs being rehomed, must have a negative *Brucella canis* test before entering Great Britain. A dog that tests positive can't be imported.

## Conditions that can void a claim

Policies come with conditions you must meet, and some matter more for an imported dog:

- **Vaccinations.** Petplan requires dogs to be kept vaccinated against distemper, hepatitis, leptospirosis and parvovirus, and won't pay for an illness the dog wasn't vaccinated against. Animal Friends won't cover diseases that could have been prevented by a vaccine a vet recommended. Rabies is the only vaccination legally required to bring a dog in from Europe, so check your dog's paperwork for the others, and ask your vet whether a booster is needed.
- **Routine care.** Petplan also requires a vet dental check at least every 12 months, plus preventative care a vet would normally recommend, such as regular worming and flea treatment.
- **Microchip.** ManyPets won't pay advertising or reward costs for a missing pet that isn't microchipped. Your imported dog will already be chipped. Make sure the chip is registered to you on a UK database.
- **Accurate answers.** Animal Friends warns that wrong information given by mistake can lead to exclusions, changes to your cover, or the policy being voided. If you don't know something, say so rather than guessing.

## Getting cover in place

A sensible sequence:

1. **Ask your rescue** whether it passes on any free introductory cover. Petplan, for example, offers 4 weeks' free cover to pets adopted through some UK rehoming centres.
2. Have the **microchip number**, the pet passport or health certificate, vaccination records and any test results from the rescue ready.
3. Compare **lifetime policies**, and read each booklet's exclusions for **leishmaniasis** and other exotic diseases, plus its vaccination and co-payment terms.
4. Take out cover **effective from the day the dog arrives**.
5. Book a **first vet check** promptly, keep the records, and discuss **screening tests** and **vaccination boosters**.
6. Re-read the policy after the **14-day waiting period** and keep a note of anything you spotted early on.

Comparing lifetime policies on price alone is a mistake — check the **annual limit, the excess, any co-payment, and the exclusions list**. Once your dog is home and insured, the focus shifts to settling in: [transport day](/guides/transport-day) covers getting those first hours right, and [the first three weeks](/guides/decompression-first-three-weeks) covers what comes after.
