An overseas rescue dog comes with a particular insurance challenge: you often don't know its full history. It may have parasites picked up abroad, an old injury that never healed quite right, or a condition that hasn't shown itself yet. Good insurance, bought at the right moment, is how you stop an unknown past from becoming an unaffordable future.

::callout{type="info"}
The most important decision isn't which insurer — it's **when**. Insure your dog from the day it arrives, before any symptom appears. A condition your dog shows signs of before cover starts is treated as pre-existing and excluded, whichever policy you choose.
::

## Why lifetime cover usually wins for a rescue

UK pet insurance broadly comes in a few shapes:

- **Accident-only** — cheapest, covers injuries but not illness. Rarely enough for a dog with an unknown medical background.
- **Time-limited** — covers a condition for 12 months from diagnosis, then stops. A problem is that chronic conditions don't respect the calendar.
- **Maximum-benefit** — covers each condition up to a fixed money limit, with no time limit, but once that limit is spent the condition is excluded forever.
- **Lifetime** — the most comprehensive: an annual limit that **resets each year**, with conditions carried over rather than excluded.

For a rescue where you can't predict what's coming, **lifetime cover** is usually the right call. The extra premium buys you continued cover for chronic conditions — allergies, joint problems, digestive issues — that a cheaper policy would drop after a year, right when you need it most.

## The unknown-history problem

Two things make imported rescues different from a UK puppy bought from a breeder:

1. **Estimated age.** Many dogs arrive with a best-guess date of birth. Insurers are used to this and will work with an estimate, but premiums step up sharply for older dogs, so it's worth getting a vet's assessment on record early.
2. **No baseline health record.** Without puppy-hood vet notes, the first UK vet visit becomes the baseline. Book a health check soon after arrival — it's good for the dog, and it documents your dog's condition at the start of cover.

## Getting cover in place

A sensible sequence:

1. Have the **microchip number** and any paperwork the rescue provided ready.
2. Take out **lifetime cover** effective from the day the dog arrives — or the earliest date the insurer allows.
3. Book a **first vet check** promptly and keep the records.
4. Read the **waiting periods**: most policies won't pay for illness in the first 14 days, so earlier is better.

Comparing lifetime policies on price alone is a mistake — check the **annual limit, the excess, and whether the vet fee limit resets each year**. Once your dog is home and insured, the focus shifts to settling in; [transport day](/guides/transport-day) covers getting those first hours right.
