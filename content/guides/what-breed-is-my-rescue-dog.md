It's the first question almost everyone asks about an overseas rescue: *what is he?* Rescue listings give a best guess, friends offer confident opinions, and the dog in front of you looks a bit like five different things. The honest answer is that you probably can't tell by looking, and that's fine. Here's what's actually known, and how to find out more if you're curious.

## Why looks are a poor guide

Mixed-breed dogs often don't look like the breeds in their ancestry. The best evidence comes from shelters that compared staff's visual breed guesses against DNA results. In a 2018 study of 919 dogs across two US shelters, staff named at least one breed that appeared in the dog's DNA **67.7%** of the time, but got more than one breed right in just **10.4%** of dogs. The typical dog in that study had around three distinct breed signatures in its ancestry.

So when a listing says "shepherd cross" or "terrier mix", read it as a description of how the dog looks, not a statement about its genes.

## What breeds are Romanian street dogs?

Usually, no single breed. Most Romanian rescue dogs are street and shelter dogs from generations of free breeding, so asking "which breed?" often has no neat answer. What many of them *are* is something more interesting: a village dog.

## What is a Romanian village dog?

Some overseas rescue dogs may not be a mix of modern breeds at all. In places like Romania there are populations of **village dogs**: free-ranging, free-breeding dogs whose ancestors were native to that region. The DNA-testing company Embark describes them as descendants of the founding dog populations in those regions, and says dogs from the same region share specific genetic similarities even though no kennel club recognises them.

That's why Embark reports a **Romanian Village Dog** ancestry category, rather than forcing those dogs into a list of Western breeds. It describes them as varied in appearance, shaped by "what works" for survival rather than by a breed standard.

### How big do Romanian village dogs get?

Embark gives a typical size range for Romanian Village Dogs:

| | Height at the shoulder | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Males** | 14–22 in (about 36–56cm) | 25–55lb (about 11–25kg) |
| **Females** | 13–20 in (about 33–51cm) | 20–42lb (about 9–19kg) |

That's medium-sized, and a long way from Romania's native shepherd breeds below, whose standards start at around 60cm at the shoulder. Individual dogs vary a lot, so treat it as a rough guide, not a prediction.

## The native breeds behind the look

Some rescue dogs may also carry the ancestry of their country's native breeds. Romania has four native livestock guardian breeds from the Carpathian mountains: the **Carpathian**, **Mioritic**, **Bucovina** and **Raven Shepherds**. They're large dogs, bred over centuries to guard flocks. Their official standards describe them as calm and stable, and as guardians: they mention wariness of strangers and a very strong bark.

## What a DNA test can (and can't) tell you

A dog DNA test compares your dog's DNA with a reference database of breeds and populations. That makes the database the thing to check:

- **It can only find what it has samples for.** If a test has no reference samples for a regional breed or population, your dog's ancestry may be matched to the nearest breed it *does* know. Check the test's current breed list for your dog's country before you buy.
- **Some tests cover the region.** Wisdom Panel has breed pages for the Romanian Mioritic and Raven Shepherds, and Embark reports Romanian Village Dog ancestry.
- **Breed isn't behaviour.** Even an accurate result tells you about ancestry, not about how your dog will behave. Your dog's own history, especially life on the street or in a shelter, matters too.

::callout{type="info"}
Testing is optional. It's a fun way to learn more about your dog, and it can be a good conversation with your vet about breed-related health. But you don't need to know what your rescue is to give them a great life.
::
