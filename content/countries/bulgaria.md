Bulgaria, like Romania, is an Eastern European country with a large population of shelter and street dogs, rehomed to the UK through registered rescues.

## Getting a Bulgarian dog to the UK

Bulgaria is an EU country, so the entry rules are the standard microchip, rabies vaccination, 21-day wait and tapeworm treatment — see the [rabies and 21-day guide](/guides/rabies-titre-test-timeline). Dogs generally travel by road across Europe, or by air.

## The health tests that matter

::callout{type="warning"}
Disease risk is where country of origin genuinely matters, and it's health information — treat this as general and confirm the right tests with your own vet. Some infections have long incubation periods, so screening on arrival and again months later is often advised.
::

As an Eastern European country, Bulgaria shares Romania's health picture, with **Brucella canis** the headline concern — it's considered endemic in Eastern Europe, and ESCCAP UK & Ireland has called for testing of dogs imported from endemic countries to be made compulsory. A full imported-dog blood panel (Brucella, Leishmania, Ehrlichia, Babesia, heartworm) is worth discussing with your vet.

## The dogs

Bulgarian rescue dogs are predominantly mixed-breed shelter and street dogs of every size and age, rather than any particular breed type.
