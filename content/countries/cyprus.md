Cyprus is the furthest of the five countries from the UK, and as an island it has no road route — dogs travel by air or sea. Its rescues rehome mixed-breed street and shelter dogs.

## Getting a Cypriot dog to the UK

Cyprus is an EU country, so the entry rules are the standard microchip, rabies vaccination, 21-day wait and tapeworm treatment — see the [rabies and 21-day guide](/guides/rabies-titre-test-timeline). Because it's an island, expect transport by air (or sea) rather than the road hauls common from mainland Europe.

## The health tests that matter

::callout{type="warning"}
Disease risk is where country of origin genuinely matters, and it's health information — treat this as general and confirm the right tests with your own vet. Some infections have long incubation periods, so screening on arrival and again months later is often advised.
::

Cyprus has a Mediterranean climate, so the profile leans towards sandfly- and tick-borne "Mediterranean diseases" — most notably **Leishmaniasis**, alongside **Ehrlichiosis, Babesiosis and heartworm**. ESCCAP UK & Ireland recommends screening imported dogs for these exotic pathogens; confirm what your dog has been tested for and plan follow-up testing with your vet.

## The dogs

Cypriot rescue dogs are predominantly mixed-breed street and shelter dogs. The individual dog's history and temperament matter far more than country of origin.
