Greece has a large stray-dog population, and its UK-facing rescues rehome a mix of street dogs and shelter dogs of all shapes and sizes.

## Getting a Greek dog to the UK

Greece is an EU country, so the entry rules are the standard microchip, rabies vaccination, 21-day wait and tapeworm treatment — see the [rabies and 21-day guide](/guides/rabies-titre-test-timeline). Dogs typically travel by road-and-ferry or by air.

## The health tests that matter

::callout{type="warning"}
Disease risk is where country of origin genuinely matters, and it's health information — treat this as general and confirm the right tests with your own vet. Some infections have long incubation periods, so screening on arrival and again months later is often advised.
::

As a Mediterranean country, Greece's profile leans towards sandfly- and tick-borne "Mediterranean diseases" — most notably **Leishmaniasis**, alongside **Ehrlichiosis, Babesiosis and heartworm**. ESCCAP UK & Ireland recommends screening imported dogs for these exotic pathogens; ask your rescue which tests have been done and agree follow-up testing with your vet.

## The dogs

Greek rescue dogs are predominantly mixed-breed street and shelter dogs. As always, the individual dog's temperament and history matter far more than any national generalisation.
