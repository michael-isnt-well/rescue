Spain is the closest of the five countries to the UK, and its rescue scene is distinctive: it's dominated by **galgos** (Spanish greyhounds) and **podencos**, sighthounds used for hunting.

## Getting a Spanish dog to the UK

Spain is an EU country, so the entry rules are the standard microchip, rabies vaccination, 21-day wait and tapeworm treatment — see the [rabies and 21-day guide](/guides/rabies-titre-test-timeline). Being closer, transport is often road-and-ferry rather than the longer hauls from Eastern Europe.

## The health tests that matter

::callout{type="warning"}
Disease risk is where country of origin genuinely matters, and it's health information — treat this as general and confirm the right tests with your own vet. Some infections have long incubation periods, so screening on arrival and again months later is often advised.
::

Spain is a Mediterranean country, so the profile leans towards sandfly- and tick-borne "Mediterranean diseases." Reputable Spanish sighthound rescues routinely test dogs before UK adoption for **Leishmaniasis, Babesiosis, Heartworm and Ehrlichiosis** — diseases that occur in Mediterranean countries but are not endemic in the UK. Ask which of these your dog has been screened for, and confirm follow-up testing with your vet.

## The dogs

Galgos are used for hare coursing and are often abandoned or killed after the hunting season; podencos are bred to hunt rabbits and small game. Both are described by rescues as adapting well to home life, though some are wary after harsh treatment. Non-sighthound mixed-breed dogs are rehomed from Spain too.
