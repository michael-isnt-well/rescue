Project Galgo is listed on the Charity Commission register for England and Wales as charity no. 1197487 (registered in January 2022). According to its website, it is "a UK registered charity dedicated to the rescue, rehabilitation and rehoming of Galgos, Podencos, Salukis and other sighthounds", working "alongside trusted rescue organisations in Spain and the Middle East". The rescue says it is "run entirely by volunteers" and that it does not rehome dogs outside the UK. Its Saluki dogs come from Bahrain, which is outside the countries this site covers; this profile concentrates on its Spanish dogs.

## The fee and what it covers

According to its adoption page, the rescue's adoption fees are "£750 for a Galgo or Podenco and £800 for a Saluki". The rescue says this "includes the purchase of the dog's Project Galgo safety kit and the purchase or 3 month loan/use of a Tractive GPS tracker for the first three months". It says that for senior dogs aged 10 and over it offers "a reduced adoption fee, which is considered on an individual basis".

The rescue also publishes a typical cost breakdown for each Galgo and Podenco, which lists:

- adoption fee paid to the Spanish shelter: £250–£300
- DEFRA-approved transport to England/Wales, including documentation: £500 (£550 to Scotland)
- Project Galgo safety kit: £60
- Tractive GPS Tracker: £59–£69
- one-year Tractive subscription: £120
- Brucella Canis test: from £100

The rescue says this adds up to "approximately £989–£1,099" per dog, and that "we do not ask adopters to cover the full cost of bringing a dog safely to the UK". It says it meets the shortfall through fundraising. The page does not say in a single sentence whether the £750 fee is intended as an all-in price that includes transport, so this profile records that field as not stated. It also says the Brucella Canis test "is payable separately by the adopter", starting from approximately £100 and varying by vet practice.

On timing, the rescue says that for a dog travelling directly from Spain the fee "must be paid in full on the day the adoption is confirmed, or in advance of their journey where requested", and that for a dog already in UK foster it requires "the signed adoption contract and full adoption fee within 24 hours" of verbal confirmation. It says that in exceptional circumstances it can accept the fee in two consecutive instalments.

## Who they rehome to

- **Where:** the rescue says it rehomes "throughout the UK, including England, Wales, Scotland and the Isle of Wight". It says its foster homes are across the country and adopters "must be willing to travel to the dog's foster home to meet them before adoption".
- **Gardens:** the rescue says that "if your property has a garden, the fencing must be appropriate for the individual dog" and that for many dogs it requires "fencing of approximately 6 feet", with exceptions assessed individually. It says it does not accept electric or sheep-wire fencing. It does not state a policy on homes without a garden.
- **Hunting homes:** the rescue says "we do not rehome our dogs to households where dogs are used for hunting".
- **Equipment and training:** the rescue lists a mandatory safety kit (an approved three-point escape-proof harness, a house collar with tag and a lead) and says dogs must wear a Tractive GPS tracker "for at least the first three months following adoption". It also says it does not permit crates, retractable leads, slip leads or aversive training equipment. The adoption form says all dogs must stay on lead for the first six months, and some for life.
- **Children and cats:** the adoption page does not state a policy. The adoption form asks whether children visit the home regularly and whether the applicant owns cats.

## The process

The rescue says the process is: an application, a homecheck, confirmation of the outcome, a discussion of suitable dogs, and then confirmation of the adoption once the agreement is signed and the fee paid. It says the homecheck "will either be carried out by a volunteer visiting your home or, where appropriate, via video call", and that it will also require identification. It says it cannot reserve a dog on the basis of an application alone.

The rescue says that dogs are tested for Mediterranean and vector-borne diseases in Spain, and that dogs with a Leishmaniasis diagnosis are adopted under a "Leishmaniasis Disclosure and Care Agreement". It also says all adopters are "required to arrange annual 4DX tests". See our guides for general background on health checks for imported dogs, and ask the rescue about the specifics for your dog.

The rescue's website does not state how often its dogs travel from Spain.

## After you adopt

The rescue says: "Following adoption, we offer a free behavioural support package for 3 months after adoption via two of our Trustees". It also says that if you are unable to continue caring for your dog, it asks you to "contact us to make arrangements for the dog to come back into foster care with us".
